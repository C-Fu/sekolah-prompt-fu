# 7 Major Features of Moon

To divide the Moon into continent-like areas, planetary geologists look at major topographical boundaries, crust thickness, and massive impact basins. Scientifically, the Moon is divided into three geochemical "terranes," but if we want to map the surface geographically into 6 or 7 distinct macro-regions—similar to Earth's continents—we can group it by its major geological provinces.

Here is how you can split the Moon into 7 distinct "continents":

### The Near Side (Facing Earth)

**1. Oceanus Procellarum (The Western Ocean)**

The largest single feature on the Moon, this vast, irregular volcanic plain dominates the western edge of the near side. Unlike other lunar seas, it isn't confined to a single circular impact crater. It spans over 1,600 miles (2,500 km) across and is characterized by high concentrations of heat-producing elements.

**2. The Imbrium Basin (The Northern Plains)**

Dominated by Mare Imbrium (the Sea of Showers), this massive circular basin forms the right "eye" of the Man in the Moon. It is ringed by the Moon's most spectacular mountain ranges, including the Montes Apenninus, which rise higher than the Himalayas.

**3. The Eastern Maria (The Tranquility Cluster)**

A distinct interconnected network of dark basaltic plains on the eastern side, including Mare Tranquillitatis (where Apollo 11 landed), Mare Serenitatis, and Mare Crisium.

**4. The Near-Side Southern Highlands**

The intensely cratered, bright, rugged terrain that takes up the bottom half of the face we see from Earth. It contains some of the Moon's oldest visible crust and is highlighted by Tycho, a massive crater with bright "rays" extending hundreds of miles across the landscape.

**5. The Near-Side Northern Highlands**

A smaller, highly degraded band of ancient crust that separates the northern maria (like Imbrium and Frigoris) from the North Pole.

### The Far Side (Facing Away from Earth)

**6. The Far-Side Highlands (Feldspathic Highlands Terrane)**

This is the largest continuous landmass on the Moon, making up almost the entirety of the far side. Because the crust here is significantly thicker than on the near side, ancient magma couldn't easily pierce through to the surface. As a result, it almost completely lacks the dark volcanic "seas" and is instead a chaotic, unbroken expanse of overlapping craters.

**7. The South Pole-Aitken Basin**

One of the largest, oldest, and deepest recognized impact craters in the entire solar system. Dominating the southern far side, it spans 1,550 miles (2,500 km) wide and is up to 5 miles (8 km) deep. The impact that created it was so massive it punched through the crust, exposing rocks from the lunar mantle, making it geologically distinct from the rest of the Moon.

# 7 Major Features of Mercury

Here are the short descriptions for the 7 major features and geological areas on Mercury:

### 1. Caloris Planitia (Caloris Basin)

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~30.5°N, 160.2°W | Diameter: ~1,550 km

- **Description:** Mercury’s dominant geological landmark. Caloris is one of the largest impact basins in the Solar System, ringed by concentric mountain ridges over 2 km high. Its floor is paved with smooth, volcanic plains that cooled and fractured into a distinctive spiderweb of grabens known as *Pantheon Fossae*.

### 2. Borealis Planitia (Northern Volcanic Plains)

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~73°N, 80°W | Extends across the high northern latitudes

- **Description:** A vast, smooth volcanic expanse encircling the north polar region, analogous to lunar maria. The lava flows here occurred after the period of heavy bombardment, burying earlier impact craters beneath deep sheets of flood basalt up to several kilometers thick.

### 3. Rembrandt Basin

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~33.2°S, 88.2°E | Diameter: ~715 km

- **Description:** The second-largest well-preserved impact basin on the planet, discovered late during the MESSENGER flybys. Rembrandt displays a unique combination of both radial compressional wrinkle ridges and concentric extensional troughs, preserving a record of Mercury's planetary cooling and contraction.

### 4. Beethoven Basin

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~20.8°S, 123.6°W | Diameter: ~643 km

- **Description:** A heavily degraded, ancient multi-ring impact basin. Unlike Caloris, Beethoven was not filled by later volcanic activity; its rim and interior remain deeply pitted by secondary cratering, making it an essential reference marker for Mercury's intermediate cratering age.

### 5. Rachmaninoff Basin

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~27.6°N, 57.6°E | Diameter: ~306 km

- **Description:** A striking peak-ring basin with an inner ring and one of the lowest volcanic floor elevations on the planet. Its interior hosts distinctive, bright volcanic plains that appear to have erupted relatively late in Mercury's geological history, indicating prolonged episodic volcanism.

### 6. Raditladi Basin

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~27.3°N, 119.1°E | Diameter: ~258 km

- **Description:** A remarkably pristine peak-ring basin showing minimal subsequent crater damage, suggesting it is relatively young (likely under 1 billion years). Inside its concentric inner ring lies a network of extensional grabens and unusual shallow, rimless depressions known as *hollows*.

### 7. The Antipodal "Weird" Terrain (Chaotic Terrain)

- **Coordinates / Extent:** ~30°S, 20°E | Antipodal (exact opposite side) to Caloris Planitia

- **Description:** A bizarre, fractured landscape of disrupted hills, broken ridges, and jumbled massifs spanning hundreds of kilometers. It was formed when seismic shockwaves from the titanic Caloris impact traveled through and around the planet, focusing at the exact opposite hemisphere and shattering the crust.

# 7 Major Features of Venus

1. **Ishtar Terra (The Northern Continent)**
- **Location:** ~65°N to 75°N, 330°E to 60°E | Highland Plateau

- **Description:** One of Venus’s two major continent-sized highland masses, roughly the size of Australia. It is dominated by the elevated volcanic plateau **Lakshmi Planum**, which is walled in by towering orogenic belts including **Maxwell Montes**—the highest point on Venus rising nearly 11 km above mean planetary radius.
2. **Aphrodite Terra (Equatorial Highland Continent)**
- **Location:** ~10°N to 15°S, 60°E to 160°E | Equatorial Highland

- **Description:** The largest continent-like landmass on Venus, stretching nearly halfway around the equator (roughly comparable in length to Africa). It consists of heavily deformed crustal plateaus (tessera terrain) like **Ovda Regio** and **Thetis Regio**, deeply fractured by major extensional rift chasms.
3. **Beta Regio (Volcanic Rise & Rift Province)**
- **Location:** ~25°N, 282°E (~78°W) | Volcanic Rise / Hotspot

- **Description:** A premier highland volcanic swell standing 4 to 5 km above the surrounding plains. Driven by an active mantle plume, it is centered along the deep **Devana Chasma** rift canyon and crowned by massive shield volcanoes including **Theia Mons** and **Rhea Mons**.
4. **Lada Terra (Southern Coronae & Flow Highlands)**
- **Location:** ~60°S to 75°S, 0°E to 60°E | Southern Highland

- **Description:** The least rugged of the three highland *terrae*, Lada Terra is characterized by vast effusive lava flooding and an extraordinary concentration of massive **coronae** (circular tectonic collapses formed by mantle upwelling), including the huge Quetzalpetlatl Corona.
5. **Atalanta Planitia (Deep Northern Lowland Plain)**
- **Location:** ~45°N to 65°N, 140°E to 200°E | Lowland Basalt Basin

- **Description:** A vast, sub-kilometer depression forming one of the deepest circular lowland volcanic basins on Venus. Analogous to terrestrial ocean basins or lunar maria, it represents large-scale crustal downwelling paved over by thousands of square kilometers of smooth basaltic lava.
6. **Sedna Planitia (Volcanic Channel Plain)**
- **Location:** ~30°N to 48°N, 320°E to 355°E | Volcanic Flood Plain

- **Description:** An extensive volcanic lowland north of Eistla Regio, carved by anastomosing thermal-erosion lava rivers (*canali*) that stretch for hundreds of kilometers across radar-smooth basalt flows.
7. **Mead Crater (Largest Impact Basin)**
- **Location:** ~12.5°N, 57.2°E | Multi-ring Impact Basin (~280 km)

- **Description:** The largest confirmed impact structure on Venus. Because Venus’s super-dense atmosphere incinerates smaller meteoroids and prevents minor craters, Mead represents an extraordinarily rare, well-preserved peak-ring impact basin with a concentric outer fault scarp and smooth volcanic melt floor.

# 7 Major Features of Mars

- **Olympus Mons (The Solar System's Tallest Volcano)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~18.6°N, 226.2°E (133.8°W)
  
  - **Profile:** A colossal shield volcano rising roughly 21.9 km above the Martian datum—nearly three times the height of Mount Everest. It spans over 600 km across, with an outer basal scarp up to 8 km high and a complex caldera collapse summit 80 km wide.

- **Tharsis Rise (The Great Volcanic Bulge)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~0°N, 250°E to 280°E
  
  - **Profile:** A massive continent-sized tectonic swell elevated up to 10 km above the surrounding terrain. It hosts the three giant Tharsis Montes shield volcanoes (Ascraeus Mons, Pavonis Mons, and Arsia Mons) and represents the largest concentration of volcanic mass on the planet.

- **Valles Marineris (The Grand Chasm System)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~10°S, 260°E to 310°E (centered ~290°E)
  
  - **Profile:** The largest canyon system in the Solar System, slicing across Mars's equatorial belt for over 4,000 km. It reaches depths of up to 7 km and widths of 200 km, formed by extensional crustal rifting as the neighboring Tharsis bulge expanded.

- **Hellas Planitia (Hellas Impact Basin)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~42.5°S, 70.5°E
  
  - **Profile:** A giant, elliptical impact crater in the southern cratered highlands spanning approximately 2,300 km in diameter. Reaching depths of 7.1 km below the datum, it is the lowest topographic point on Mars; the atmospheric pressure at the basin floor is high enough to occasionally permit liquid water under specific temperature conditions.

- **Syrtis Major Planum (Basaltic Shield Plain)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~9.9°N, 70.0°E
  
  - **Profile:** A dark, low-relief basaltic volcanic shield identifiable even through small ground telescopes. It is stripped of reflective dust by persistent local winds, revealing unweathered pyroxene-rich volcanic rock surrounding two central caldera depressions (Nili and Meroe Paterae).

- **Elysium Planitia & Elysium Mons (Eastern Volcanic Province)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~25°N, 147°E
  
  - **Profile:** Mars's second-largest volcanic region, anchored by the 14-km-high shield of Elysium Mons. It features vast flood lava plains, shallow grabens (Cerberus Fossae), and some of the youngest basalt flows identified on Mars.

- **Vastitas Borealis (Northern Lowland Plains)**
  
  - **Coordinates:** ~60°N to 90°N (encircling the North Pole)
  
  - **Profile:** The extensive, smooth plains occupying the Martian northern hemisphere, situated 4 to 5 km lower in elevation than the rugged southern highlands. This stark elevation contrast (the **Martian Crustal Dichotomy**) forms the floor of what many planetary scientists hypothesize was an ancient northern ocean basin.
